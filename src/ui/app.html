Thinking-budget tiers

    
Light / Extended / Heavy are implementation policies, not universal model guarantees. Accuracy vs. reasoning tokens is roughly logarithmic with a task-dependent knee; past the knee, overthinking appears — the model second-guesses correct answers, drifts off constraints, and burns latency.

    
TierBudgetRoute here whenTypical controls

    
On retrieval, style, and format tasks extended thinking yields ~0 gain and increases variance — route Light. For a fixed compute budget, k samples at medium thinking usually beats 1 sample at max thinking when a verifier or voting rule exists; without one, prefer serial depth.

How MetaForge works

    
You give it four things it can't guess. It gives back a prompt that encodes everything six frontier models independently agreed on about getting maximum output quality per token.


    

      
The pipeline

      
1 — Classify. Your task text is scanned for shape: linear, search, aggregation, verification, or exploration. Shape determines topology, and topology is where most of the quality difference lives. Tree-of-Thoughts turns 4% into 74% on search-shaped tasks and is pure waste on linear ones.

      
2 — Route the thinking budget. Risk, constraint count, verifier availability, and novelty map to Light / Extended / Heavy. Uniform Heavy is the single most common way teams burn money for negative returns.

      
3 — Select topology. If a programmatic verifier exists, generate-and-verify dominates everything else. Otherwise the compiler falls through the selection heuristic to ToT, GoT, Plan-and-Execute, Self-Consistency, or a single expert with recursive reflection.

      
4 — Assemble by position. Invariants at the head, task and freshest evidence at the tail, and an authoritative constraint reprise after any untrusted block. Recall is highest at the beginning and end of context with a measurable trough in the middle, so the least critical material sits in the body.

      
5 — Install anti-hallucination rails. A groundedness pass labeling every claim SUPPORTED / DERIVED / ASSUMED / UNSUPPORTED with a delete-or-downgrade rule, an adversarial pass, and a structured insufficiency object so the model has a legal way to say "I don't have enough" instead of confabulating.

      
6 — Bound the reflection loop. Depth 1–3 depending on whether a real verifier exists. Ungrounded self-critique past depth 2 flips correct answers to incorrect ones, so the loop is capped deliberately and revisions must preserve verified-correct spans verbatim.

      
7 — Adapt to the target model. Claude gets XML isolation and prefill. GPT gets strict Structured Outputs and no hand-written CoT. Gemini gets systemInstruction plus responseSchema. Open-weight models get flattened schemas and decode-time grammars.

      
8 — Stamp provenance. Every compile emits a context hash plus KB and engine versions, so any prompt in the wild traces back to the exact compiler that produced it (R19).

    


    

      
Where the token savings come from

      
    
        
  • Right-sized reasoning. Routing Light instead of blanket-Heavy on format and retrieval work removes 30k+ reasoning tokens per call at no accuracy cost.
    • 
        
  • No decorative scaffolding. ToT and multi-agent structures are only emitted for shapes that benefit.
    • 
        
  • Bounded reflection. Capped where returns go flat or negative instead of an open-ended "keep refining".
    • 
        
  • Artifact offload. Payloads over 2k tokens become a pointer plus a 200-token abstract.
    • 
        
  • Constrained output. A schema costs +0–10% tokens and removes the retry loop that costs 100%.
    • 
        
  • Explicit stop conditions. Prevents the model padding a finished answer.
    • 
      

      
Where the hallucination reduction comes from

      
    
        
  • A legal way to fail. The insufficiency object means the model needn't invent something to satisfy the request.
    • 
        
  • Claim-level labeling. SUPPORTED/DERIVED/ASSUMED/UNSUPPORTED surfaces confabulation before it ships.
    • 
        
  • Domain-specific NEVERs. Targeting each domain's actual failure mode, not generic caution.
    • 
        
  • Verification honesty. "Never claim a gate passed unless you executed it" closes the most common agent lie.
    • 
        
  • Trust boundaries. Typed tags plus a constraint reprise substantially reduce indirect prompt-injection success.
    • 
        
  • Calibrated abstention. On high-stakes work with no programmatic gate, the prompt asks for a number the model would bet on.
